My Path to Network Science

My path into network science grew from a deep curiosity about how simple physical interactions give rise to complex collective behavior. During my Bachelor's degree in Physics at Shiraz University, I focused on computational astrophysics, developing a strong foundation in statistical mechanics, numerical modeling, and large-scale simulations.

Joining the MSc in Physics of Data at the University of Padua, I discovered that the concepts of statistical physics and information theory provide a powerful framework for deciphering complex networks. Under the supervision of Prof. Manlio De Domenico, my Master's thesis investigated generalized thermodynamics and optimization in mammalian connectomes—solidifying my decision to dedicate my research to network science.

Today, as a PhD Candidate in the CoMuNe Lab at the University of Padua, I study functional fragility in complex networks—exploring how communication flow and information pathways respond to structural perturbations.

Functional Fragility & Network Resilience

Question: Which connections and pathways are essential for communication between selected parts of a network, and how does their disruption affect overall function?

Context: We move beyond structural connectivity to measure set-to-set information flow using Laplacian diffusion, spectral entropy, and pathway perturbation analysis.

Methodology & Focus

Using Laplacian density matrices, we compute propagation time and received information between node subsets. By evaluating functionality loss under edge removals, we compare identified critical pathways against standard topological metrics in empirical and synthetic networks.

Information Dynamics in Mammalian Connectomes

Question: How does structural connectome wiring govern information flow and evolutionary clustering across species?

Context: Brain networks optimize physical wiring against information integration. We applied density matrix representations and information-theoretic distance metrics to mammalian connectomes.

Methodology & Highlights

Implemented Simulated Annealing and Particle Swarm Optimization to align topological connectome models with diffusion dynamics. Results presented at NetSci-X 2024 demonstrated information-theoretic distances unraveling species clusters closely mirroring phylogenetic trees.
